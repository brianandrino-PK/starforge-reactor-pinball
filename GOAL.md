# Product Goal: Cosmic Pinball

## Walkthrough

The player opens the site and immediately sees an original, full-height cosmic pinball cabinet with a short controls overlay. They can start with a keyboard, mouse, gamepad, or touch screen. Pulling and releasing the plunger launches a ball into a lively table whose bumpers, slingshots, lanes, ramps, targets, and flippers react with satisfying motion, light, sound, and score feedback.

The first few shots teach the table without a tutorial wall: lit inserts show an active skill shot, target banks spell a short objective, and ramps visibly advance toward multiball. As the player gains control, timed combos, escalating multipliers, and a central creature or reactor objective create a route from survival to mastery. A drain is disappointing but legible; ball-save, remaining balls, bonuses, and the next useful objective are always clear.

A complete run builds toward a short wizard-mode climax. When the run ends, the game explains the score, important achievements, and personal best, then makes restarting instant. The initial release is a polished single original table inspired by the depth and responsiveness of modern browser pinball—not a copy of another game's code, name, story, artwork, music, or exact layout.

## Capability questions

1. Can a first-time player understand how to launch and operate both flippers within ten seconds?
2. Does the ball respond consistently to flipper timing, impact angle, gravity, and table geometry?
3. Can a skilled player deliberately aim for lanes, ramps, target banks, and the central objective?
4. Does each meaningful collision provide synchronized visual, audio, and score feedback?
5. Can the player tell which objective is active without pausing play or reading dense instructions?
6. Does every new ball offer a clear, timing-based skill-shot opportunity?
7. Can the player make measurable progress toward multiball through repeated accurate shots?
8. Does multiball materially change the pace, scoring opportunity, and risk of the game?
9. Can alternating shots create time-limited combos that reward controlled play?
10. Can target completion raise a visible bonus multiplier without making scoring opaque?
11. Can the central table objective be attacked repeatedly for jackpots and a larger narrative payoff?
12. Does the table communicate ball save, extra ball, locked balls, multipliers, and remaining balls at a glance?
13. Can the player nudge the table within strict limits, with tilt preventing unlimited abuse?
14. Does a drain preserve and explain the correct end-of-ball bonus before the next ball begins?
15. Can the player reach a distinct wizard mode by demonstrating mastery across several table systems?
16. Does wizard mode combine prior mechanics into a short, readable climax rather than mere visual noise?
17. Can a complete run be played with keyboard, mouse, touch, or a common gamepad?
18. Does the table remain playable and legible on both desktop and portrait-oriented mobile screens?
19. Can sound and music be muted independently without affecting gameplay feedback?
20. Does the game pause or safely suspend when the browser tab loses focus?
21. Is a personal best preserved locally without requiring an account or transmitting player data?
22. Can a player restart quickly after game over without reloading the page?
23. Does the game maintain smooth, deterministic-enough physics under ordinary browser frame-rate variation?
24. Can future tweaks change table geometry, scoring values, objectives, colors, and tuning without rewriting the whole game?

## Scope boundaries

- One original pinball table for the first release.
- No copied code, names, characters, artwork, music, voice lines, or exact table layout from Alien Escape Pinball or another game.
- No accounts, cloud saves, global leaderboard, multiplayer, ads, purchases, or paid-provider calls.
- No backend, database, user tracking, or personal-data collection.
- No production release until the Owner explicitly approves it after reviewed runtime evidence.
- Additional tables, a level editor, and social features remain future possibilities, not current scope.
